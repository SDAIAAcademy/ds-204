The choice to write down

A fixed threshold or a fixed list length

Fixed list length

Always fits the advisers. The weakest name on the list varies: in a hard intake, trainees at higher risk are left out.

Fixed threshold

Equal risk is treated equally across intakes. The list overruns or underuses capacity, and depends on the probabilities staying calibrated.

  • Either can be defended; the fund chose the list length
  • Write down which rule runs, and who may change it

The fund's reason: capacity is a number of plans. A mixed rule is common in practice: a fixed length, with a floor below which nobody is listed. Whatever the rule, it is part of the model card, because the deployment team will implement exactly what is written.

The idea

A calibrated score keeps a promise: of those given 0.3, three in ten leave

  • Calibration is about what the numbers mean
  • Ranking is about their order
  • A model can be good at one and bad at the other
  • It matters when the number is shown to an adviser, compared with a break-even, or multiplied by a cost

How to measure it

Sort the trainees by score and cut them into ten groups. In each: the mean score (expected) beside the share that left (observed). One summary number: the Brier score, the mean squared gap between score and outcome.

Deciles and not finer bins: with 9,276 trainees each decile has 928 and a stable observed rate. The Brier score rewards both ranking and calibration, so it is read together with the decile table and never alone. Sources: scikit-learn user guide, Probability calibration.

The repair

A calibrator is a small model from score to probability

Sigmoid

A logistic regression on the log-odds of the score: two numbers, a shift and a stretch. Works with few rows; cannot bend.

Isotonic

A rising step function. Takes any rising shape; needs more rows, and trainees on one step become tied.

  • Fitted on rows the scoring model was not trained on: here the 2024 cohort
  • Tested on rows neither has seen: the 2025 cohort
  • For the weighted model the sigmoid's shift is −2.0; for the champion, −0.2

From Lab 4.2. A calibrator fitted on training rows learns to trust scores that have partly memorised their outcomes: the same reason a training score is not logged. In scikit-learn, CalibratedClassifierCV does the fitting inside folds; the lab does it by hand so that the two numbers of the sigmoid can be read.

Errors are not symmetric

Two errors, two different harms

Missed

A trainee leaves and was not on the list. Nobody called. The harm falls on the trainee. Measured among those who left: the miss rate, 0.62 for the 2025 list.

Listed without need

A trainee on the list would have completed anyway. An adviser's hour is spent; the trainee gets a friendly call. Measured on the list: one minus precision.

  • The first harm leads the audit
  • The question: is the miss rate the same for everyone?

Which error matters most depends on the action. Here the action is support, so being missed is the harm. If the action were a penalty, being listed without need would lead. Decide this before looking at any table. Sources: SDAIA Principles of AI Ethics, the fairness principle.

The last teaching session: the page that leaves with the model, and the framing of the capstone.

The hand-over

A model card is the one page that travels with the model

Measured: filled from results

Intended use · data · validation scheme · results on held-out rows · calibration · errors by subgroup.

Written: no number writes them

What the model is not for · where the scores do not hold · who decides · what the next team needs.

  • Its readers were not in the room: an adviser, an auditor, the deployment team
  • Every number in it can be traced to a run

The card is short on purpose. If it needs three pages, it will not be read by the people who need it. In Lab 4.4 the measured half is assembled from the reference results; the participants write the other half. Sources: Mitchell and others (2019).

What the card admits

The results section says what was not shown

  • Champion 0.529 on the folds; against the logistic rung +0.005, interval −0.010 to +0.019: an advantage is not shown
  • New trainees 0.491, returning trainees 0.578: the headline is an average
  • Expected 13.7%, observed 13.5% overall; four points too low at new providers
Why write it down. The next team may need a model an adviser can read, or one that is cheaper to run. The card tells them a simpler one does as well within the uncertainty.

A card that reports only its best number invites the reader to find the rest. The three sentences here come from Labs 2.4, 3.1 and 4.2. They make the card more useful and its author more credible.

The written half

A useful limit names a situation and what to do in it

WeakUseful
"May not generalise to new data.""At a provider that opened after training the probabilities are about four points too low: use the rank, not the number."

The sentence to keep

The score puts a name on a list; a person decides

  • An adviser decides whether and how to contact a trainee
  • An adviser may add any trainee who is not on the list
  • No trainee is refused, ranked for funding or told a score
  • Providers are not ranked by it

What the score does not answer

It says who is likely to leave. It does not say whether a support plan helps, or whom it helps most. That needs a comparison of supported and unsupported trainees, which the fund has not run.

The first column restates the note of the decision file from Day 1. The second is the one causal remark of the course: the figure "one in four" for the effect of a plan is the fund's assumption. A model that predicts leaving is not a model of what support changes. Sources: SDAIA Principles of AI Ethics, the principles of humanity and of accountability and responsibility.
